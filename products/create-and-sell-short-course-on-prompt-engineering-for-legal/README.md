## Course Overview  

**Target Audience:** Lawyers, paralegals, legal analysts, and law‑firm staff who want to use large language models (LLMs) reliably for everyday legal work.  

**Prerequisites:** Basic familiarity with LLMs (e.g., having used ChatGPT, Claude, or similar). No programming required; optional Python snippets are provided for those who wish to automate prompts via API.  

**Learning Outcomes:** By the end of the course you will be able to:  

1. Explain the core principles of prompt engineering and why they matter for legal accuracy.  
2. Design, test, and refine prompts for four high‑value legal tasks: legal research, contract review, memo/brief drafting, and case‑law summarization.  
3. Apply a simple evaluation rubric to measure prompt quality (relevance, completeness, hallucination risk, and style).  
4. Build a repeatable prompt‑pipeline that can be shared with teammates or embedded in a firm’s knowledge‑base workflow.  

**Course Structure:** 5 lessons, each delivered as a drip‑email (or Gumroad “section”) containing:  

- A 5‑7 minute video script (ready to record).  
- A slide deck in Markdown (can be exported to PowerPoint/Google Slides).  
- A hands‑on practice exercise with detailed instructions, expected output, and a model solution.  
- A short quiz (3‑question multiple choice) to reinforce key points.  

**Total Estimated Time:** 2.5 hours of video + 1 hour of exercises + 30 min of quizzes ≈ 4 hours.
